faq:
- question: Do I need 4K resolution for video calls, or is 1080p enough?
  answer: 1080p is enough for almost everyone. Zoom, Teams, and Google Meet all compress video down well below 4K before it reaches the other person, so the extra resolution rarely shows up on a call. 4K webcams are worth it mainly for recording content you'll edit afterwards, like YouTube videos, where you can crop into the frame without losing quality.
- question: Will a webcam actually look better than my laptop's built-in camera?
  answer: Almost always, yes. Even an entry-level dedicated webcam usually has a larger sensor and better low-light handling than the small camera built into a laptop lid, and sits at a more flattering eye-level angle on a monitor or stand rather than looking up your nose from a low laptop screen.
- question: Do I need a webcam with a built-in microphone?
  answer: Not if you already have a decent headset or standalone microphone, which will sound better in almost every case. A webcam's built-in mic is there for convenience — one less thing to plug in — and is fine for casual calls, but anyone doing client calls or presentations regularly will get clearer audio from a separate mic.

A laptop's built-in camera is usually grainy, poorly angled, and struggles the moment the room isn't brightly lit. A dedicated webcam clipped to a monitor fixes the angle and the image quality in one go, and you don't need to spend much to notice the difference.

## What actually matters

- **Resolution vs real-world use** — 1080p at 30fps covers virtually every video call platform; 4K only pays off if you're recording footage to edit later, since calls compress the image down regardless.
- **Low-light performance** — most home offices aren't brightly lit, so a webcam with good auto-exposure and light correction matters more than headline resolution for how you actually look on camera.
- **Field of view and autofocus** — a wide lens keeps you framed if you lean in or move around; autofocus avoids the soft, hazy look of a fixed-focus lens once you shift position.
- **Microphone and privacy shutter** — a built-in mic is a convenience, not a necessity, if you already use a headset; a physical privacy shutter is a simple, reliable way to cover the lens when it's not needed.
