faq:
- question: What adjustments actually matter most on an office chair?
  answer: Seat height and lumbar support matter most for day-to-day comfort, followed by armrest height and seat depth. Reclining tension and headrests are nice extras but not essential for most people working at a desk.
- question: Do I need a mesh back or is padded fine?
  answer: Mesh backs breathe better and suit warmer rooms or people who run hot, but some find padded backs more comfortable for long sitting. Neither is objectively better — it's largely personal preference once basic lumbar support is present.
- question: How long should a decent office chair actually last?
  answer: A well-built chair used daily should comfortably last 5-8 years before the gas lift, castors, or foam noticeably degrade. Cheaper chairs under heavy daily use often show wear within 1-2 years, which is worth weighing against the upfront price difference.

A chair without real lumbar support turns into a genuine problem after a few months of full-time use, not just a comfort issue. Here's what to check, and which ones hold up.

## What actually matters

- **Genuine adjustable lumbar support** — not just a curved back panel, but something that actually moves to fit your spine.
- **Seat height and depth adjustment** — matters more than most buyers expect, especially for shorter or taller-than-average users.
- **Breathable material** if you run warm or work in a poorly ventilated room — mesh backs help noticeably here.
- **A proper gas lift and castors rated for your flooring** — hard floors need different castors than carpet to avoid marking or poor rolling.
