- question: Do I need a separate keyboard and mouse if I use a laptop stand?
  answer: Yes, in practice. Raising the laptop to put the screen at eye level also lifts the built-in keyboard and trackpad out of a comfortable typing position, so a laptop stand works best paired with an external keyboard and mouse. Without them you're trading neck strain for shoulder and wrist strain.
- question: Will a laptop stand actually keep my laptop cooler?
  answer: Usually, yes, though how much depends on the stand. Any design that lifts the laptop off a flat desk surface lets air reach the underside vents instead of the laptop drawing warm air back in off the desk. Open-frame aluminium stands with cutouts do this most effectively; fully enclosed vertical stands help less since the vents are still partly blocked.
- question: What's the difference between a portable stand and a desktop stand?
  answer: A portable stand folds flat and is light enough to carry daily, usually plastic or thin metal, built for a laptop bag rather than permanent use. A desktop stand is heavier and sturdier, often solid aluminium, designed to sit on one desk and hold position under a monitor or external keyboard setup without folding away.

A laptop balanced flat on a desk puts the screen too low and blocks the vents underneath it. A stand fixes both at once, and which one suits you depends more on whether it needs to travel with you than on price.

## What actually matters

- **Screen height over styling** — the main job of any stand is lifting the screen closer to eye level; a few centimetres of extra height makes a bigger difference to neck strain than material or finish.
- **Stability under typing** — if you'll type directly on the laptop rather than an external keyboard, check the stand doesn't flex or rock; a wobbly riser is worse than no riser at all.
- **Airflow underneath** — an open-frame design with cutouts lets air reach the laptop's underside vents; a solid, fully enclosed base traps heat instead of dispersing it.
- **Portable vs fixed** — folding plastic or thin-metal stands pack into a bag for commuting or hybrid work; solid aluminium desktop stands are heavier but sturdier for a single permanent setup.
