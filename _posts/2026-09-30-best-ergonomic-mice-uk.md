faq:
- question: Is a vertical mouse actually better for wrist pain, or is that a myth?
  answer: For many people, yes. A vertical mouse holds your hand in a natural "handshake" position instead of the fully pronated (palm-down) position a standard mouse forces, which reduces strain on the forearm muscles that a flat mouse works hardest. It's not a guaranteed fix for existing conditions like RSI or carpal tunnel, but it's a genuinely different load pattern worth trying if a flat mouse causes discomfort.
- question: Vertical mouse or trackball — which should I choose?
  answer: A vertical mouse still moves with your whole hand and arm, just at a friendlier wrist angle, so it feels closer to a normal mouse to learn. A trackball stays still on the desk while your thumb or fingers move the ball, removing arm movement entirely, which some people find more comfortable but takes longer to get used to. If in doubt, a vertical mouse is the easier switch.
- question: Will a vertical or ergonomic mouse work fine with a normal Windows or Mac laptop?
  answer: Yes. Every mouse in this guide connects over standard Bluetooth, a USB wireless dongle, or USB-C/USB-A cable and works with both Windows and macOS without extra drivers, though some brands offer optional software for customising buttons and DPI.

A flat mouse forces your forearm to twist palm-down for hours at a stretch, and that's the strain most people are actually trying to fix. Here's what genuinely helps, and which mice do it well.

## What actually matters

- **Wrist angle over gimmicks** — a proper vertical mouse (typically around 57 degrees) or a trackball changes the actual load on your forearm; cosmetic "ergonomic" shaping on an otherwise flat mouse does far less.
- **Connection type that suits your setup** — Bluetooth and USB dongle both work fine day-to-day; a dongle is usually lower-latency, Bluetooth saves a USB port and pairs with more devices.
- **Button count and placement** — extra thumb buttons for back/forward or DPI switching are genuinely useful once you're used to them, but not essential if you just want relief from wrist strain.
- **Learning curve** — switching to vertical or trackball takes a few days to feel natural; budget mice let you try the concept cheaply before spending more.
