faq:
- question: Do I need loud clicky switches for the office, or something quieter?
  answer: Stick to quiet tactile or linear switches (often labelled "brown" or "red", or marketed as "silent") for a shared office or frequent video calls. Loud clicky switches feel great solo but are genuinely disruptive to colleagues sitting nearby or on the other end of a call.
- question: What does hot-swappable actually mean, and do I need it?
  answer: A hot-swappable keyboard lets you pull out individual switches and replace them without soldering, so you can change the feel or fix a faulty key yourself. It's a genuinely useful feature if you think you'll want to experiment, but not essential if you're happy with the switch the keyboard ships with.
- question: Is a wireless mechanical keyboard reliable enough for daily office work?
  answer: Modern wireless mechanical keyboards using Bluetooth or a 2.4GHz dongle are reliable for everyday typing, with most offering weeks of battery life per charge. The main trade-off versus wired is a very small amount of input latency, which is unlikely to matter outside of fast-paced gaming.

A cheap membrane keyboard that goes mushy after a year, or a gaming board that sounds like a typewriter on a call, both miss the point for office use. Here's what actually matters, and which ones get the balance right.

## What actually matters

- **Switch type matched to your environment** — quieter tactile or linear switches for shared offices and video calls, clicky switches only if you work alone or don't mind the noise.
- **Wired vs wireless** — wireless keeps the desk tidier and works well for most typing, but check battery life and whether it supports a low-latency 2.4GHz dongle if you're sensitive to lag.
- **Layout and size** — full-size keyboards keep the number pad, while tenkeyless (TKL) or 75% layouts free up desk space for a mouse without losing many keys.
- **Build quality and keycaps** — a solid frame and PBT keycaps resist shine and wear far better over years of daily use than cheap ABS plastic.
